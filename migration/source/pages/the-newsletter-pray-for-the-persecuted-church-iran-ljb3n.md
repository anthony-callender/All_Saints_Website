During the conflict in Ukraine, Barnabas Aid has been helping suffering Christians with food (three-months' supply) for 560 families, along with hygiene kits for 320 families. Donations also covered house repairs for five families. Pray that God will continue to sustain and encourage His Church in Ukraine.
Pray for our brothers and sisters in Ukraine.
Week of 9/27/26: Mali, West Africa
Al Qaeda-affiliated militants (terrorists) in Mali recently increased the amount of territory they hold. The ultimate goal of these Islamist terrorists is to take over several countries in West Africa. While there are not a lot of Christians in Mali (about 2%), everyone is precious to Jesus, and all need our prayers.
Pray for our brothers and sisters in Mali, West Africa.
Week of 9/20/26: Chibok, Borno State, Nigeria
Praise God that 6,000 Christians in this Nigerian state have recently been reached with food through Barnabas Aid. The recipients are those severely affected by Islamist terrorism in northeastern Nigeria. Because of violence and instability, farmers have not been able to grow food. Pray for these precious people who have suffered so much.
Pray for our brothers and sisters in Chibok, Borno State, Nigeria.
Week of 9/13/26: Yemen
A young woman from a Muslim background was recently converted to Christ. Now she prays for the conversions of her family members. Christian converts in Yemen are hated by the Muslim majority as apostates. In addition millions suffer from severe humanitarian needs such as food shortages. Pray for Christians who risk even their lives for the sake of their faith.
Pray for our brothers and sisters in Yemen.
Week of 9/6/26: North Korea
North Korean authorities boast that they have virtually eliminated all religion. The experts in South Korea do not believe this: "We know there are still strong Christians who are worshiping in small groups or as individuals." The Church cannot be defeated by the powers of darkness, but we must continue to pray for them as they endure unthinkable hardships for the Name of Christ.
Pray for our persecuted brothers and sisters in North Korea.
Week of 8/30/26: The Philippines
Two islands in the Philippines were the worst-affected in the region by unprecedented tropical storms in the fall of 2025. These storms caused widespread destruction and hundreds of deaths. Barnabas Aid provided urgently needed food and clean drinking water for 2,110 Christian families. Pray for these families to be spiritually strengthened as they continue to recover from their ordeal.
Pray for our suffering brothers and sisters in the Philippines.
Week of 8/23/26: Iraq
Due to remarriage, it is not unusual for someone born as a Christian in Iraq to have to change their official religion from Islam back to Christianity. Normally, this is extremely difficult, but recently the court in Iraq allowed a young woman to have her court records amended so that her papers now say she is Christian. Join her in praising God for this unusual ruling.
Pray for our brothers and sisters in Iraq who are under similar circumstances.
Week of 8/16/26: Iran
As you can imagine, the Church in Iran is suffering greatly. They are under political pressure, fear, and surveillance. When Christians are caught, especially pastors and leaders, they are usually put in the notorious Evin Prison, from which most are either severely persecuted or killed.
Pray for our brothers and sisters in Iran.
Week of 8/9/2026: Pakistan
Most Christians in Pakistan live way beneath the poverty line — many suffer to get adequate food.
Those who give to Barnabas Aid provide monthly dollars which help the Christian community in Pakistan survive. Many are what is known as “Brick Kiln Workers,” where families are bonded to the owners for generations. Barnabas Aid has gradually over the years been able to free some of them. It is nearly impossible to express the level of their gratitude.
Pray for our brothers and sisters in Pakistan.
Week of 8/2/26: China
Over the past several years, the Chinese Church has become subject to increasing restriction and regulation, including church leaders, who must “love the motherland, support the leadership of the Communist Party of China, and practice the core values of socialism.” As a result, the Three-Self Protestant Church enjoys less freedom, while unofficial “house churches” are targeted by the authorities. Many pastors have been tortured, imprisoned, punished with severe physical work, and even killed outright. Pray that the church will be able to successfully navigate these sevee legal restrictions.
Pray for our brothers and sisters in China.
Week of 7/26/26: Sri Lanka
Christians are a marginalized and persecuted group in Buddhist-majority Sri Lanka. Local authorities and Buddhist extremists, including monks, perpetuate the false idea that Christian worship is forbidden without permission. Service and prayer meetings are routinely shut down, even in private homes. Converts are particularly targeted. Pray for the Lord to protect pastors and congregations, and for courage and perseverance for all Christians
Pray for our brothers and sisters in Sri Lanka.
Week of 7/19/26: Egypt
Many Christian families in Egypt struggle with poverty and rising living costs, especially in remote and underserved areas where access to food is limited. Recent gifts through Barnabas Aid brought relief. Pray that such acts of love will strengthen faith, develop community, and shine as a witness to their neighbors of Christ’s compassion. Ask the Lord to continue sustaining the Church in Egypt, enabling them to remain steadfast.
Pray for our brothers and sisters in Egypt.
Week of 7/12/26: Nepal

Aid projects through Barnabas Aid provide schooling and vocational training and shelter for at-risk girls and women who have suffered abuse. Pray that this work will be a blessing to marginalized Nepali Christians, and that it would strengthen the Church.
Pray for our brothers and sisters in Nepal.

Pray for our brothers and sisters in Bangladesh.
Week of 6/28/26: Afghanistan
The Taliban government in early 2026 strengthened legal discrimination against religious minorities. Only followers of the Hanafi school of Islamic jurisprudence are recognized as “true Muslims” — those who practice any other religion or belief are now labeled as being “innovators” or “heretics.” This code prescribes death for those who share or hold “beliefs contrary to Islam.” Christians and others will be killed if their faith is discovered. Pray that the Lord will strengthen the spirit of His people as they endure such severe opposition. It is not known how many Christians there are in Afghanistan, as they must remain in strict hiding.
Pray for our brothers and sisters in Afghanistan.

Week of 5/24/26: Algeria
Pastor Youssef Ourahmane has been arrested for leading his protestant church in Algeria. He converted from Islam in 1977 and has now led a church for three decades. His conviction is for “illegal worship,” even though Algeria’s constitution says it protects free speech and religious freedom. However, Algeria has other repressive laws that violate the rights of Christians and other religious minorities. He is appealing his conviction before the Supreme Court of Algeria and is only the latest of many to be convicted. Although his church has been closed, he continues to support his congregation. Alliance Defending Freedom is arguing his case at the Supreme Court.
Pray for Pastor Youssef and the other Christians in Algeria, and pray for the work of the Alliance Defending Freedom.

There were 72 people killed in Plateau State in Nigeria over Easter.
The killings, fires, and brutalities were carried out by the Fulani Muslim herdsmen,
ostensibly
to obtain more land for their cattle. Thousands of Nigerians also carried out a peaceful march to Government House in Jos, the capital, to call upon the government to provide protection before these events take place, not afterward. The President is Muslim and the government seems not to care about the death toll. Pastor Amos Mohzo said, “We are tired of these mass killings. Enough is enough!”
Pray for our suffering brothers and sisters in Nigeria.
Week of 4/27/25: Saudi Arabia
Saudi Arabia is the birthplace of Islam and contains the cities considered to be the most holy to the religion: Mecca and Medina. Sharia (Islamic law) is strictly enforced and leaving Islam is punishable by death. Christians are banned from publicly practicing their religion. Saudi Arabia's inhabitants who believe must be secret Christians. Pray that they will be strong in their faith and Jesus will watch over them and keep them safe.
Pray for our brothers and sisters in Saudi Arabia.
Week of 4/20/25: Venezuela
There has been an intense government crackdown on non-governmental organizations (NGOs). In August 2024, 1,650 churches and Christian NGOs were forced to close. Pray that the Holy Spirit will draw near to Christians in Venezuela.
Pray for our brothers and sisters in Venezuela.
Week of 4/13/25: The Sahel
The “Sahel” is composed of several nations: Mali, Burkina Faso, and Niger as well as other countries. The Church in Mali is
very small
. These Christians are under a high-alert level of food insecurity, meaning that famine has become a severe possibility. The situation is largely driven by conflict caused by Islamist terrorists who are active in the region. Their food insecurity is such that 1.4 million people in Mali are on course to suffer “acute food insecurity” and 120,000 are at risk of “emergency levels.” Ask the Lord to provide for the hungry and bring peace to these troubled lands.
Pray for our brothers and sisters in Mali, Burkina Faso, and Niger as well as other countries in the region of the Sahel.
Week of 4/6/25: Uzbekistan
Recently the church in Uzbekistan has benefited from regular training opportunities for church leaders. Their ages range from 65 to 15 and they are involved in various ministries — teachers, worship leaders, prayer group and house church leaders, children and youth workers, as well as evangelists. Give thanks for these leaders and their training. Pray that each participant in the training will effectively apply what they learn as they build up the Body of Christ.
Pray for our Uzbekistan brothers and sisters.
Week of 3/30/25: Pakistan
Many Christians in Pakistan suffer under the charge of “blasphemy” against Islam, particularly against Mohammad and the Quran. Most of these Christians are illiterate. Many Christians in Pakistan are under the yoke of debt and bonded labor. Their livelihood is working as brick kiln workers whose loans — taken out at times of crisis from their owners — have led to them and their families being trapped almost as slaves. Give thanks that a Christian organization, Barnabas Aid, has been able to free many of these families. Give thanks for those who have been freed and those who have been occasionally acquitted in the courts.
Pray for our Pakistani brothers and sisters.
Week of 3/23/25: Kazakhstan
Many Christians in Kazakhstan are mostly poor and are often denied employment because of their faith. Many are converts from Islam who have to keep their faith secret, even from their own families. There is often no money or materials for outreach, which is strictly monitored, or to repair or build new churches. Yet the Kazakh church's love for Jesus means they remain grateful and passionate about Him.
Pray for our Kazakh brothers and sisters.
Week of 3/16/25: Nigeria
Nigerian Christians suffer persecution across their nation, but particularly in the north with the incursion of militant Islamic terrorists. Recently Northern Plateau State has been particularly hard hit. Several churches and Christian schools have been damaged. A school building collapsed. killing 22 students and severely injuring others. At least one church and school are without electricity in a particularly remote part of this state. Also in Niger State a church building was burned down by unidentified arsonists. These situations plus kidnappings of pastors and church leaders by militant Islam make being a Christian in Nigeria require extraordinary courage, strength, and faith.
Pray for our brothers and sisters in Nigeria.
Week of 3/9/25: Myanmar (Burma)
The ongoing conflict in Myanmar is the result of the Tatmadaw (Myanmar military). They are Buddhist and have caused endless suffering to the church for years. They are particularly against certain Christian tribal groups (the Karen for example). They bomb churches and “chase” IDPs (internally displaced persons) into the jungles, killing and injuring many. Recently, in Mon Hla village, one whole side of a church building was destroyed in a bombing raid, as was most of the village. Residents were twice forced to flee as drones were spotted searching for and chasing them.
Pray for our brothers and sisters in Myanmar (Burma).
Week of 3/2/25: Libya
Libya, a country on the Mediterranean in North Africa, is deeply politically unstable with two rival governments. One is internationally recognized in Tripoli; the other is in the east. Both claim authority to run the country's central bank, a stand-off which could lead to economic collapse.
Only the Lord knows how many Christians are in Libya, but there are a small number of indigenous converts from Islam plus refugees from sub-Saharan Africa, whose goal is to cross the Mediterranean into Europe. Many drown attempting to make the crossing.
Pray for stability for our brothers and sisters in Libya.
Week of 2/23/25: Sudan
Sudan has undergone a severe civil war which began in April 2023 and continues to the present time. In early October 2024, 95 Christians were arrested by the “Sudan Armed Forces” (SAF) in River Nile State in north Sudan. These Christians (25 women, 54 children, and 16 men) were accused of collaborating with the “Rapid Support Forces” (RSF). At the current writing, it is said that the women and children have been released but not the men.
Pray that our brothers and sisters will recover from their ordeal. Pray for the protection of many other Christians displaced by the conflict in Sudan.
Week of 2/16/25: Sri Lanka
Sri Lanka, an island nation at the base of India, is suffering from severe inflation which has improved recently. However, poverty remains widespread.
One-quarter
of households experience food insecurity and consume an inadequate diet. Eight percent of the population are Christian and are among the poorest members of society. In the past 10 years or so they have experienced persecution, even those who are of the traditional well-established Catholic and Orthodox churches.
Pray for our brothers and sisters in Sri Lanka.
Week of 2/9/25: Maldives
Pray for the persecuted church in the Maldives, a group of islands in the Indian Ocean and one of the most repressive Muslim-majority countries in the world. Their constitution states that "no law contrary to any tenet of Islam shall be enacted in the Maldives.” Non-Muslim worship is prohibited even by visitors to this island paradise. Their citizens are assumed to be Muslim. There are a few indigenous converts who must remain secret believers or face reprisals from the state and from their families.
Pray for our hidden brothers and sisters in the Maldives, but give praise to God that He knows each one by name!
Week of 2/2/25: Bhutan
The country of Bhutan is a Buddhist-majority South Asian nation in which Christians are not allowed to meet publicly for worship. Churches are not recognized officially by the government, they cannot own property, and evangelism is illegal. They can usually worship in private, however.
Pray for our brothers and sisters in Bhutan.

Pray for the hungry persecuted church around the world.

Week of 1/12/25: Afghanistan
As you probably know, Afghanistan under the auspices of the Taliban is under increasing restrictions through its interpretation of Sharia (Islamic) law. Forty percent of children under 5 are so malnourished that their growth is stunted. The scale of the humanitarian disaster taking place under the Taliban is extreme. All women (including Christians) are required to disguise their voices and completely cover their bodies, faces, and hands and are forbidden to go out without a male relative accompanying them. Christian women and girls are particularly vulnerable. All Christians - if it becomes known that they have converted from Islam - will likely be killed for apostasy if their faith is discovered. In addition, they cannot gain employment and must remain in strict hiding.
Pray for our brothers and sisters in Afghanistan.
Week of 12/29/24: Bangladesh
Bangladesh is in political turmoil after the Prime Minister fled the country in August. Christians and other religious minorities are facing violence from Islamists, particularly Islamist political parties. They are attempting to use the political vacuum to gain power. Church leaders are seeking assistance from the interim Prime Minister so that the Christians and others will face no deprivation, injustice, or oppression.
Pray for our brothers and sisters in Bangladesh and for an end to the unrest.
Week of 12/22/24: Egypt
While generally Christians fare reasonably well in Egypt, some areas experience greater persecution, especially churches in Upper Egypt. In Beni Suef, a church building was destroyed in a massive fire. While the cause continues to be unknown, the building had five floors, offices and a library, and it also housed three schools. No children were injured, but 10 adults were badly burned and hospitalized because of the blaze. Pray for God’s deep care and providence after the loss of so many important facilities. Pray that the church can eventually be rebuilt.
Pray for the persecuted church in Upper Egypt.
Week of 12/15/24: Iraq
There are fewer and fewer Christians in Iraq. They leave because of the instability of the country and the fear of persecution. Before the US-led invasion of Iraq, there were about 1.5 million Christians in Iraq. Sectarian tensions meant that only 700,000 were left by the time the Islamic State seized a third of the country. Christians were among those subjected to violence and most were displaced from their homes. Now it is estimated that only 150,000 to 200,000 are left and this number continues to fall.
Pray for our brothers and sisters in Iraq who have suffered so much.
Week of 12/8/24: Pakistan
Pakistan has extremely harsh “blasphemy laws” which encompass any so-called blasphemy against the Quran or Mohammad or Allah (the Islamic god). The law is very broad and Muslims take advantage of this against Christians. Those accused are often treated violently by mobs, and imprisoned for years, often without trial.
The accusations are usually very flimsy. For example, in two recent cases four people were arrested for destroying pages of the Quran. All four were illiterate and had no idea they had pages of the Quran.
Pray for our imprisoned brothers and sisters in Pakistan.

Week of 11/17/24: Democratic Republic of the Congo
In the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), the northern part (North Kivu) is the worst affected area. At least 84 people were murdered by fighters from Islamic State Central Africa Province (ISCAP) in July and August. At least five more were killed in nearby Itrui province. This violence has been going on for several years. Terrorists also killed at least five others in August and burned down more than 50 houses. The fear is that Islamic violence is spreading to new areas of the country. Pray for the bereaved and that the Lord will restrain this evil.

The Islamists who killed them and claimed responsibility were from a West African terror group affiliated with Al Qaeda. After killing the 26 men, the attackers set fire to houses and stole livestock. The Jihadists now control more than 40% of the nation's territory. Week of 11/3/24: Mozambique
“Islamic State Mozambique Province” in 2020 abducted and abused Christians, in particular Amina, a 27-year-old woman. By the grace of God, she kept her faith, wasn’t killed as others were, and was released in 2023. Pray for her and others who survived as they continue to heal from their extreme trauma. Since 2017, 6,000 have been killed by the Islamists. Many of those were Christians. Elections occurred last week in this country where the north is experiencing an Islamist insurgency. Pray that people who desire peace will be elected.
Week of 10/27/24: Pakistan
Several issues are critical to the persecuted church in Pakistan.
1) Many young Christian women and girls (as young as 13 or 14) are kidnapped and forced to marry significantly older Muslim men. Once married and often with children it is virtually impossible to get them away from their husbands and divorced. The state usually takes the side of the Muslim men.
2) Normally Christians are illiterate. They are often accused by Muslims of destroying pages from the Quran, which, of course, in most cases they cannot read. They are then arrested, imprisoned and accused of blasphemy. Their punishment is legal under Pakistan Penal Code 295-B. These actions terrify the Christian community because such action is often followed by violence by Muslim extremists. Pray for the Persecuted Church in Pakistan.

Week of 9/1/24: Nigeria
Sporadic killings, persecution, kidnapping and abductions are part of the reality of today's Nigeria. This week we will cover another of the numerous disturbing issues and current happenings, especially in Christian Communities across Nigeria.
This ugly situation and senseless killing began as a movement by Fulani Herdsmen parading their cattle across Southern Nigeria seeking vegetation for their cattle. Little did the Nigerian population know that a greater agenda was for Muslim groups from North Africa to overrun and destroy Christian communities with the pretense of breeding and grazing cattle in various parts of Nigeria, especially in the Northeast, Southeast, and Middle belt regions. Sadly the Fulani-led government under the Buhari administration intentionally empowered them by instituting and passing an “Open Grazing” law that gave these Fulani Herdsmen the impetus to graze at will wherever they found choice vegetation on private properties, farmlands, and family backyard gardens.
Facts established that the Fulani strategy has continued to reveal their intentions to aggressively graze their herds, burn and persecute Christians who get in their way while on this destructive mission with a large number of casualties year after year.
Pray for our Christian brothers and sisters who are simply trying to make a living or who are attempting to feed their families.
Week of 8/25/24: Myanmar (Burma)
Christians in Myanmar, especially in certain tribal areas, are under severe persecution by the Tatmadaw (Myanmar's military). They are regularly hunted down, bombed, and have their homes and churches destroyed. Frequently their pastors are killed. Groups of them flee deep into the jungle to escape torture. In the jungles they often survive by eating leaves, etc. The children suffer inordinately. This has been going on for decades. The Anglican Church has a strong presence there and welcomes short-term missions like SOMA (Sharing of Ministries Abroad). Please pray for our brothers and sisters in Myanmar.
Week of 8/18/24: North Korea
Christians in North Korea are considered to be the most persecuted in the world today.
No display of Christianity is tolerated; all North Koreans are to “worship” their leader, Kim Jong-Un. No gatherings of Christians are allowed under any circumstance and all Koreans are under strict surveillance. No Bibles or Christian books are allowed, although a few Bibles are sometimes smuggled in. If a Christian is caught praying, reading a Bible, or in any way proselytizing, they are either severely punished or imprisoned in harsh labor camps where they are usually worked to death. Please pray for our brothers and sisters in North Korea!

Week of 8/11/24: Nigeria
Christians in Nigeria have been under extreme persecution for a decade now. Although Islamic insurgence and persecution began two decades ago, the North-East, North-West, South-East and the Middle-Belt have suffered unimaginable loss in the hands of these Islamic terrorists.
The peculiarity of these attacks occur in Christian communities where these terrorists intend to take over and expand their area of influence. These Muslim Jihadists, notably called “Boku Haram” (which means “Western education is forbidden”) were reported to have infiltrated into Northern Nigeria from The Niger Republic and the Chad region. They primarily abduct and kill Christian clergy, demand millions of Naira (tens of thousands of dollars) in ransom, kidnap children of Christian faith, burn churches and houses, and decimate villages and communities at will.
In 2014, they kidnapped about 276 teens from a Christian school in Chibok, Borno State in Northern Nigeria; forced them into marriages and also forced them to recant their Christian faith or be beheaded. Over the years, about 100 of these young women have escaped or been rescued from this terrorist group and returned with two or three children born in captivity.
Please cover our precious brothers and sisters with prayer.
